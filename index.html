Our Mission

Research takes money.
Money takes us.

Childhood cancer receives less than 4% of federal cancer research funding. For the kids behind that statistic, the gap is not academic — it is the difference between a clinical trial that exists and one that doesn't.

Rebels Fight Cancer is a student-led 501(c)(3) nonprofit answering that gap. We organize events, fundraisers, and awareness drives across our chapters, then funnel proceeds directly to the National Pediatric Cancer Foundation, a research nonprofit pushing experimental treatments toward kids who need them now. Our founding chapter is at Great Neck South High School. New chapters are opening as students bring it to their own schools.

We don't want to grow large. We want to give all of it away.

Raise

Chipotle nights, popcorn sales, candygrams, and community runs.

Direct

Every dollar routed through our NPCF team page — no overhead.

Mobilize

500+ hand-made cards sent to hospitalized kids with Project: LIFE and the GNS Pre-Med Club.

About the Board

The students behind it.

Co-Founder, Co-President & Outreach Lead

Aayan Alwani

Aayan is a junior at Great Neck South High School. He co-founded Rebels Fight Cancer to turn a long-running interest in cancer research and biology into something that reaches kids who actually need it. As outreach lead, he runs everything that involves expanding the organization, reaching out to local businesses that host our fundraisers, and the general work of getting more students across the country engaged with pediatric cancer.

Two ways in.

Whether your school already has a chapter or you want to bring one there yourself, there is a path. Both start with a DM.

Join an existing chapter

If your school already has a Rebels Fight Cancer chapter, we want you on the team. Show up to events, take on a leadership role, or just help run the next fundraiser. No application, no minimum hours, no fluff.

Not at your school yet? You can bring us. We will send the chapter starter kit, our NPCF partner intros, and the playbook we used in our first year so you do not have to figure it out from scratch.
